I am an M.S. student in Energy & Petroleum Engineering at the University of Wyoming, advised by [Dr. Soheil Saraji](https://www.uwyo.edu).

My research focuses on the computational mechanics of granular particles interacting with deformable surrounding media — specifically contact behavior, interface deformation, and coupled fluid transport across scales. I model proppant grain behavior under compressive loading between parallel rock fracture walls using **solids4Foam** (OpenFOAM-based). This involves constructing structured O-grid meshes, implementing elasto-plastic Mohr–Coulomb rock constitutive models, and validating stress distributions against Hertz contact theory. The longer-term goal is coupling embedment results with single-phase fluid flow to quantify fracture conductivity changes in shale gas systems.

I also have broader interests in granular-structure interfaces, particle penetration, and load-partitioning interactions in open-source multiphysics frameworks.

Before UW, I was a full-time research intern at **KAIST** (Graduate School of Mechanical Engineering, advised by Dr. Yeunwoo Cho), where I investigated gravity–capillary wave dynamics on elastic silicone films and developed 2D ventilated supercavitation models. I also interned at **Samsung Heavy Industries**, developing neural network architectures for vessel defect classification. My undergraduate degree is in Naval Architecture & Ocean Engineering at Pusan National University (advised by Dr. Inwon Lee), where I conducted CFD simulations of vortex–wave interactions and built ANN-based structural health monitoring models.
